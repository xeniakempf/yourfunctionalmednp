Hormones

The Intersection of Nutrition & Women's Hormones

By Xenia Kempf, FNP-C · July 16, 2025 · 4 min read

For many women, hormonal health feels like a mystery. Symptoms like fatigue, weight changes, mood swings, PMS, and sleep disturbances are common—but often brushed off or treated piecemeal without addressing the root causes.

Nutrition, stress, and hormone balance are deeply interconnected. And today’s advanced testing options, like the DUTCH Test, can give us a far more comprehensive picture of what’s really going on.

Let’s explore how nutrition and hormones intersect, why understanding stress and the HPA axis is essential, and how modern testing empowers women to take a more holistic approach to health.

How Modern Testing Methods Like DUTCH Provide a Comprehensive Approach to Women's Hormones

The DUTCH Test (Dried Urine Test for Comprehensive Hormones) is a cutting-edge tool that goes far beyond a standard blood test.

Unlike traditional lab tests that measure a single hormone level at one moment, the DUTCH Test provides:

✅ Comprehensive sex hormone profiles (estrogen, progesterone, testosterone)

✅ Cortisol patterns throughout the day

✅ Metabolites that show how hormones are processed and cleared

✅ Insight into adrenal function and the HPA axis

✅ Markers of nutrient deficiencies like B12 and glutathione

This big-picture view helps practitioners and clients see not just what’s off—but why.

Understanding the HPA Axis, Cortisol, and Nutrient Depletion

One of the most critical systems impacting women’s hormones is the HPA axis —the network between the Hypothalamus, Pituitary Gland, and Adrenal Glands.

✅ Cortisol, your primary stress hormone, is produced by the adrenal glands.

✅ When you’re under chronic stress, cortisol levels may become elevated, flattened, or dysregulated.

✅ High or dysregulated cortisol can disrupt other hormones, including progesterone, estrogen, DHEA, and even thyroid hormones.

Chronic stress also burns through nutrients:

✅ B vitamins (especially B6, B12, folate)

✅ Magnesium

✅ Vitamin C

✅ Zinc

✅ Amino acids for neurotransmitter production

Low nutrient reserves make it even harder for your body to maintain balanced hormones.

How Stress Impacts Hormonal Balance and Weight Loss

For women trying to manage weight or improve hormonal symptoms, stress is often the missing link.

✅ Chronic stress elevates cortisol, which can increase abdominal fat storage.

✅ High cortisol disrupts blood sugar balance, driving cravings and insulin resistance.

✅ Stress can suppress reproductive hormones, leading to irregular cycles, PMS, or low libido.

✅ Sleep disruption from stress makes everything worse—hormone imbalances, appetite regulation, and mood.

You cannot address weight loss or hormone health without looking at stress management.

The Need for a Holistic Approach to Health

Women’s health is complex—and there’s no one-size-fits-all solution.

A holistic approach means considering:

✅ Nutrition: Are you getting enough nutrients to support hormone production and detoxification?

✅ Stress management: Are you balancing work, family, and self-care?

✅ Mental well-being: How are anxiety, mood, and emotional health impacting your physical health?

✅ Movement: Are you exercising in a way that supports—not stresses—your body?

✅ Sleep: Are you getting restorative sleep to allow hormones to rebalance overnight?

This big-picture view empowers women to make sustainable changes that actually work.

How DUTCH Testing Offers Deeper Insights

DUTCH testing shines because it doesn’t just show a hormone level —it shows patterns and pathways.

✅ How your cortisol rises and falls through the day

✅ How your body metabolizes estrogen (helping assess breast cancer risk)

✅ Whether your progesterone levels are sufficient in the luteal phase

✅ Testosterone production and clearance

✅ Organic acid markers for nutrient status

With these insights, you can:

⭐ Personalize nutrition and supplement plans

⭐ Target stress management strategies

⭐ Support liver detoxification pathways for hormone clearance

⭐ Adjust exercise intensity to match adrenal health

Final Thoughts

Hormonal health isn’t just about balancing estrogen or progesterone—it’s about understanding the whole system.

Stress, nutrition, sleep, mental health, and movement all play a role in shaping hormone balance.

Advanced testing like the DUTCH Test gives us the roadmap to understand what’s really going on and tailor solutions that work for you.
