Hair loss is a common issue that many people face, and we need to consider various internal and external factors that could be contributing to the problem. The functional medicine approach emphasizes the importance of addressing the root causes of hair loss rather than just masking the symptoms. Below are the main causes of hair loss:

## 1. Nutrient Deficiencies

![A woman holding a hair brush](local:inline/main-causes-of-hair-loss-0.webp)

Nutrient imbalances are one of the most common causes of hair loss. There are several key nutrients that are essential for healthy hair growth, and deficiencies in these nutrients can lead to thinning or loss of hair. Some of the most critical nutrients for hair health include:

- **B Vitamins** : Deficiencies in biotin (B7), folate (B9), and B12 can lead to hair thinning.

- **Iron** : Low iron levels (often due to anemia) can reduce oxygen delivery to hair follicles, resulting in hair loss.

- **Zinc** : This mineral plays a critical role in cell growth and repair, and zinc deficiency can lead to hair shedding.

- **Vitamin D** : A deficiency in vitamin D has been associated with hair loss, particularly in cases of autoimmune-related hair loss, such as alopecia areata.

- **Omega-3 Fatty Acids** : These essential fats are crucial for maintaining healthy hair follicles.

I recommend assessing nutrient levels through testing and correcting deficiencies through food or supplementation to promote healthy hair growth.

## 2. Hormonal Imbalances

Hormonal imbalances are another major contributor to hair loss. Fluctuations in hormones, particularly in women, can lead to hair thinning or shedding. Some common hormonal causes of hair loss include:

- **Thyroid Imbalances** : Both hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) and hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) can lead to hair thinning. An imbalance in thyroid hormones disrupts the hair growth cycle, causing excessive shedding.

- **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)** : Women with PCOS often experience hormonal imbalances involving elevated androgens (male hormones), which can result in hair thinning, particularly on the scalp.

- **Androgenic Alopecia** : This condition is commonly referred to as male-pattern baldness, but it can also affect women. It occurs due to an excess of the androgen hormone dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which shrinks hair follicles.

- **Estrogen and Progesterone Fluctuations** : In women, hormonal shifts during pregnancy, menopause, or perimenopause can cause temporary or permanent hair loss. A drop in estrogen levels during menopause is particularly associated with thinning hair.

Addressing these imbalances through functional medicine approaches like dietary changes, lifestyle modifications, and in some cases, hormone therapy, is key to restoring hair health.

## 3. Chronic Stress

Chronic stress and elevated cortisol levels are significant factors contributing to hair loss. Elevated levels of cortisol can disrupt the hair growth cycle, leading to a condition known as **telogen effluvium** , where hair prematurely enters the resting phase, resulting in excessive shedding.

- **Stress-Induced Hair Loss** : Prolonged stress can not only trigger hair loss but also prevent new hair growth.

- **Cortisol Imbalance** : When cortisol levels are chronically elevated, it can hinder the function of other hormones and affect overall metabolic processes, which can have a detrimental effect on hair follicles.

I recommend adopting stress management techniques such as meditation, deep breathing exercises, yoga, and regular physical activity to lower cortisol levels and support hair health.

## 4. Gut Health and Inflammation

The health of your gut plays a critical role in overall well-being, including hair health. An imbalanced gut microbiome, also known as **dysbiosis** , can lead to systemic inflammation, which can negatively affect hair growth. Inflammation in the body can disrupt the proper functioning of hair follicles and even cause conditions like **autoimmune hair loss** (e.g., alopecia areata).

- **Leaky Gut Syndrome** : I cannot emphasize enough how a leaky gut—when the lining of the gut becomes compromised—can lead to toxins and undigested food particles entering the bloodstream, triggering an immune response that may impact hair health.

- **Inflammatory Diet** : Diets rich in processed foods, sugars, and unhealthy fats can exacerbate inflammation in the body and contribute to hair loss.

Restoring gut health through dietary changes (such as eliminating inflammatory foods), taking probiotics, and supporting digestion with digestive enzymes are critical strategies for improving hair health.

## 5. Environmental Toxins

Exposure to environmental toxins is another factor that can cause hair loss. Toxins, such as heavy metals (e.g., mercury, lead, and arsenic), pesticides, and chemicals found in personal care products, can accumulate in the body and affect hair follicles, causing thinning or shedding. Toxins can also interfere with the body's natural detoxification pathways, leading to greater systemic toxicity and inflammation.

- **Chemical Exposure** : Chemicals found in beauty and personal care products, such as parabens and phthalates, can disrupt the endocrine system and contribute to hair thinning. I have compiled lists of safer products on my website that are available to you, under "Resources."

I recommend reducing exposure to environmental toxins by choosing organic and non-toxic products, eating organic foods, and supporting the body's detoxification systems with specific supplements like glutathione and activated charcoal.

## 6. Blood Sugar Imbalance

Blood sugar imbalances, including insulin resistance and fluctuating blood sugar levels, can contribute to hair loss. Chronic high blood sugar can increase levels of insulin and inflammation, both of which can adversely affect hair follicles. Additionally, fluctuations in blood sugar levels may exacerbate conditions like PCOS, which is commonly linked to hair thinning.

- **Insulin Resistance** : Insulin resistance can contribute to excess androgen production, which in turn leads to hair thinning and male-pattern baldness in both men and women.

To address blood sugar imbalances, I recommend adopting a balanced, low-glycemic diet rich in healthy fats, protein, and fiber, and avoiding refined carbohydrates and sugary foods.

## 7. Aging

As we age, our hair naturally undergoes changes, including thinning and slower growth. Hormonal shifts, nutrient depletion, and changes in the body's ability to produce collagen and keratin—key proteins in hair structure—can lead to hair loss. Although aging is inevitable, supporting overall health with a balanced diet, stress management, and lifestyle changes can help slow the progression of hair loss.

By identifying underlying issues such as nutrient deficiencies, hormonal imbalances, chronic stress, gut health problems, environmental toxins, blood sugar imbalances, and aging, individuals can take a more holistic approach to preventing or treating hair loss.
