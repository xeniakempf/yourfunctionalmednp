If you live in West Texas, the Panhandle, East Texas, or the Permian Basin, you already know what it feels like to need a doctor and not be able to get one. Appointments booked out for weeks. Long drives to the nearest clinic. Rushed 10-minute visits that leave you with more questions than answers. This is not a failure of the people — it is a failure of the system. And for hundreds of thousands of Texans, **Direct Primary Care** is the way out.

## Texas Has a Primary Care Crisis — And It's Getting Worse

[Texas is one of the most underserved states in the country when it comes to primary care](https://texas2036.org/posts/physician-shortage-grows-worse-across-texas/). 228 of Texas's 254 counties are now designated health professional shortage areas — nearly double what it was just a few years ago.

That shortage is felt everywhere, but it hits hardest in cities and regions that are already underserved:

- **Midland and Odessa** — the Permian Basin's booming population has far outpaced its healthcare infrastructure. Residents routinely wait weeks for a primary care appointment, or drive hours to Lubbock or San Antonio for basic care.

- **Lubbock** — despite being a regional hub, Lubbock serves an enormous surrounding rural population that has few local options of its own.

- **Amarillo** — the Panhandle's largest city, but primary care access across the surrounding region remains critically limited.

- **Tyler** — East Texas's healthcare hub, but demand has long outstripped supply for primary care providers serving the broader region.

- **San Angelo** — a classic example of a mid-sized Texas city where a small pool of PCPs serves a wide geographic area.

- **Beaumont** — Southeast Texas continues to face access challenges that pre-date and post-date major hurricane events that further strained an already thin provider network.

The traditional healthcare system's answer to these shortages has been: wait longer, drive farther, or go to the emergency room. Direct Primary Care offers a different answer entirely.

## What Is Direct Primary Care?

Direct Primary Care (DPC) is a membership-based healthcare model that removes insurance from the equation for everyday primary care. Instead of billing insurance for each visit, you pay a flat monthly membership fee — typically between $75 and $150 per month for adults — and in return you get direct, unhurried access to your own personal physician.

No copays. No claims. No surprise bills for a routine visit. No sitting in a waiting room for an hour to spend seven minutes with a doctor who has 2,000 other patients.

DPC physicians keep intentionally smaller patient panels — typically 300 to 600 patients rather than the 2,000+ standard in a traditional insurance-based practice. That smaller panel is what makes the difference. It is what allows your doctor to actually answer your call, respond to your text, see you the same day when something comes up, and more with you when your situation requires it.

## The Benefits of Direct Primary Care

### 1. Same-Day and Next-Day Appointments

One of the most immediate benefits DPC members notice is access. When you are sick, you can reach your doctor — not a nurse hotline, not a voicemail, not a portal that gets checked in three business days. Most DPC practices offer same-day or next-day appointments for acute issues, and many offer tele-health visits that can happen within hours.

For patients in Midland, Odessa, Lubbock, and San Angelo — where waits for a PCP appointment can stretch to three, four, or even six weeks — this kind of access is transformative.

### 2. Longer Visits, Real Conversations

The average primary care visit in a traditional insurance-based practice lasts 7 to 10 minutes. That is barely enough time to address one problem, let alone a whole person's health picture.

DPC visits typically run 30 minutes. Your doctor has time to listen. Time to ask follow-up questions. Time to explain what is happening in your body and what your options are. Time to talk about your lifestyle, your stress, your sleep, your goals — the factors that drive the majority of chronic disease but almost never get addressed in a conventional appointment.

### 3. Direct Access to Your Doctor

![Doctor in scrubs with a stethoscope using a phone](local:inline/why-direct-primary-care-is-the-answer-1.webp)

DPC members typically have their physician's direct phone number or messaging contact. You can text a question about a medication. Send a photo of a rash. Get lab results explained in plain language. Have a tele-health call from your truck between job sites.

This kind of access is not a luxury. For people managing chronic conditions like diabetes, hypertension, or thyroid disease — or for parents navigating a child's health — it is the difference between catching something early and ending up in the emergency room.

### 4. Transparent, Predictable Pricing

In traditional healthcare, you rarely know what something is going to cost until the bill arrives weeks later. In Direct Primary Care, your monthly membership covers your primary care visits. Many DPC practices also offer deeply discounted rates on labs and generic medications — negotiated at wholesale prices and passed directly to members.

A lipid panel that might cost $200 through a hospital lab often costs $10 to $15 through a DPC practice's wholesale lab network. Common generic medications that cost $40 to $80 at a retail pharmacy are often available for a few dollars per month. These savings add up significantly over the course of a year, especially for patients managing multiple conditions.

### 5. Prevention-First, Root-Cause Care

Because DPC physicians are not paid per procedure or per visit, they have no financial incentive to order unnecessary tests or rush you in and out. Their incentive is your health — because healthy, satisfied members are members who stay.

This model naturally lends itself to prevention-first care. Your doctor has the time to address the habits, lifestyle factors, and early warning signs that conventional medicine never gets to. Catching pre-diabetes before it becomes diabetes. Addressing sleep and stress before they become cardiovascular disease. Getting ahead of the chronic conditions that are responsible for the majority of healthcare costs and lost quality of life.

### 6. Care That Works Alongside Your Insurance

Direct Primary Care is not a replacement for health insurance. You still need coverage for hospitalizations, specialist care, surgeries, and emergencies. But DPC dramatically reduces how much you actually need to use that insurance for everyday care.

Many patients pair a DPC membership with a high-deductible health plan — a combination that is often more affordable than a traditional low-deductible plan alone, especially when you factor in how much less frequently DPC members use emergency rooms and urgent care clinics. Some employers are now pairing DPC with an ICHRA (Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement) to offer their employees a real healthcare benefit without the cost of traditional group insurance.

## Why DPC Makes Particular Sense in Underserved Texas Communities

In cities like **Midland, Odessa, Amarillo, Lubbock, Tyler, San Angelo, and Beaumont** , the traditional primary care model is failing people in a very specific way: there simply are not enough physicians, and the ones who are there are stretched impossibly thin.

The DPC model was designed for exactly this environment. Because DPC practices have lower overhead — no large billing departments, no insurance contract administrators, no prior authorization specialists — they can operate sustainably with a smaller patient base. That makes it viable for a physician to serve a community at a scale that actually allows for real relationships and real care.

For patients in these communities, DPC means:

- Not having to drive two or three hours for a primary care appointment

- Having a doctor who actually knows your name, your history, and your family

- Getting care proactively instead of reactively — before the situation becomes an emergency

- Building a relationship with a physician who can coordinate your care across specialists, labs, and imaging

## Is Direct Primary Care Right for You?

Direct Primary Care works especially well for:

- Adults managing one or more chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disease, autoimmune conditions, metabolic syndrome)

- People who currently have no primary care physician or cannot get timely appointments

- Individuals and families with high-deductible health plans who want to reduce out-of-pocket costs for everyday care

- Small business employees whose employer cannot afford traditional group health insurance

- Anyone who wants an ongoing relationship with a physician who actually knows them — not whoever happens to be available at an urgent care clinic

It is a particularly good fit for patients in West Texas, the Panhandle, East Texas, and Southeast Texas who have been navigating a primary care shortage for years and are ready for something different.

## What to Look for in a DPC Practice

Not all DPC practices are the same. When you are evaluating whether a practice is right for you, ask:

- What is included in the monthly membership? (Visits, labs, medications, tele-health?)

- What is the physician's panel size? (Smaller panels generally mean better access)

- How quickly can I be seen for an acute issue?

- How do I reach my doctor between appointments?

- What happens if I need a specialist or hospital care — do you help coordinate that?

- Is there a long-term contract, or is membership month-to-month?

A good DPC practice will answer these questions transparently and without hesitation.
